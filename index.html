Latest AI News — Edition 1 · Monday 23 Nov 2026

The biggest AI news this week (16–22 Nov 2026), picked as future OmBot videos. The week's big theme: AI is getting cheaper fast — new models cost less while doing more. Benchmark scores below are the vendors' own numbers, not independent tests.

1. Canva finally lets you turn AI off 19 Nov 2026 · Source: 9to5Mac

Canva added two free controls for Free and Pro users: a Generative AI switch (Profile > Settings > Permissions) that hides AI features across the app, and a content control that shows fewer AI-made templates. Background Remover and Magic Grab keep working even with AI switched off.

Why it matters: you can choose a clean, human-made workspace — or turn AI back on anytime, without paying extra.

Video idea: “Canva Finally Lets You Turn AI Off — Here's the Switch”

2. Gemini now lets you keep your chats without training Google's AI 16 Nov 2026 · Source: Google blog

Google split the old “Keep Activity” setting into two controls: History (how long chats are saved) and training (whether your chats train Google's AI). You can now keep your history and still opt out of training. Note: with History off, chats are still saved for around two months.

Why it matters: this is the privacy setting every Gemini user should check.

Video idea: “Stop Gemini From Training on Your Chats (New Setting)”

3. OpenAI launches GPT-6.2 — smarter, and 20% cheaper 19 Nov 2026 · Source: PYMNTS

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra (frontier model for hard professional work) and GPT-6.2 Sol (efficient everyday model) on 17 Nov. Astra's API price fell 20% to $8 per million input tokens. Both are available to paid ChatGPT Work and Codex users and through the API.

Why it matters: the best AI is getting cheaper — professional-quality drafting, spreadsheets and analysis now cost less per job.

Video idea: “ChatGPT Just Got a New Brain: GPT-6.2 Explained”

4. Google's Gemini 4 Neon: near-top quality at half the price 19 Nov 2026 · Source: IT Daily

Google launched Gemini 4 Neon on 18 Nov: a 1-million-token context window, four adjustable thinking levels, and API pricing of $3 per million input tokens — less than half the $8 charged by OpenAI and Anthropic for their top models. It ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index.

Why it matters: you no longer need the most expensive model to get top results — the middle option is now very good.

Video idea: “Google's New Gemini Beats ChatGPT's Best on Coding — for Half the Price”

5. Microsoft launches 7 AI models in one day — including the best image editor 17 Nov 2026 · Source: Thurrott

At Ignite 2026, Microsoft announced seven MAI models built around price-performance, including MAI Image 2.7 — top-rated for image editing, beating GPT Image 2.6 at one-fifth the price — and MAI Code 2, live in GitHub Copilot.

Why it matters: AI is splitting into cheap specialists, so picking the right tool for each job matters more than picking the biggest model.

Video idea: “Microsoft Just Made 7 AI Models — One Edits Images Better Than ChatGPT”

6. The company behind Claude is now worth about $1.5 trillion 18 Nov 2026 · Source: Investopedia

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude chatbot, priced its IPO at $83 a share — above its $70–$78 target — raising about $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record. Shares began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on 19 Nov and closed day one up 17%.

Why it matters: the AI tools beginners use every day are now run by public companies worth trillions — heavily funded, here to stay, and watched by shareholders.

Video idea: “The AI Behind Claude Is Now Worth $1.5 Trillion — What It Means for You”

7. AI just got dramatically cheaper: one new model costs 7 cents 19 Nov 2026 · Source: PYMNTS

On 16 Nov, Alibaba released Qwen4-Max with roughly a 60% reduction in average cost per task, and Tencent launched Hy4 Flash at $0.07 per million input tokens — less than one-hundredth the price of OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra.

Why it matters: automating a task with AI keeps getting cheaper, so jobs that weren't worth automating last year are worth it now.

Video idea: “AI Got 100x Cheaper in One Week — Here's Why”

Copy-paste prompts (from video 06)

The 4-part formula: WHO + WHAT + FOR WHOM + HOW. Full prompts with examples in the prompt library on GitHub.

❌ Before — a vague prompt

“Write an Instagram caption for my coffee shop.”

Result: a generic caption that could belong to any shop — no price, no audience, no tone, nothing to copy.

✅ After — the same ask, with the formula

WHO + WHAT + FOR WHOM + HOW — see Prompt 1 below. Same coffee shop, but now the answer is ready to post.

Example result: “Slow morning? Warm cup, zero rush. Kopi Gula Aren — Rp 18,000, made for busy office days. Open 08:00–17:00.”

Shop caption:
You are my coffee shop assistant. Write 3 Instagram captions for Kopi Gula Aren, Rp 18,000 (about $1.10), for busy office workers. Friendly tone, under 20 words each, no hashtags. End each caption with our opening hours: 08:00-17:00.
Customer reply:
You answer chats for Kopi Pagi, a small coffee shop. Reply to the customer below in the same style as this example. Example: Customer: "Kak, masih ada Kopi Gula Aren?" Reply: "Masih ada kak, hari ini 40 cup. Mau berapa cup? 😊" Keep it short, max 3 sentences. Do not invent prices, stock, or discounts. If you do not know, say so and ask. Customer message: [paste here]
Fix-it follow-ups:
That's too long. Cut it to [n] words and keep the main point.
Make it sound more like me. Here's something I wrote: [paste].
What did you assume that I didn't tell you?
Give me 3 different versions so I can pick.

How the videos are made

Every video is short on purpose — under 90 seconds, one idea, no jargon. Watch one, try the idea right away, and come back for the next. No camera, no talking head: a small round robot named OmBot explains, with pictures that change as the idea changes.

Curious how they're built? The animations are drawn with code (Python + PIL, 60 fps) and narrated with text-to-speech, then joined into one full video with voice. The full pipeline is open in this repo’s pipeline folder.
